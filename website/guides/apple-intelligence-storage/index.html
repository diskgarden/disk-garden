How much storage does Apple Intelligence use on a Mac?

More than Apple's own figure suggests, and on macOS 27 considerably more. Here's what people have actually measured, how to see the number on your own Mac, and what you can realistically get back.

What Apple says, and what people measure

Apple's stated requirement for macOS 27 is up to 14 GB of on-device storage on Macs with an M3 chip or later that also have at least 12 GB of unified memory, and up to 8 GB on other Apple Intelligence-capable Macs. On earlier versions of macOS the models were smaller — roughly 4–7 GB.

The measured figures run higher. MacRumors collected these in September 2026:

  • 20.67 GB on an M4 Pro Mac mini — measured by MacRumors, on a Mac in the 14 GB tier.
  • 22.42 GB on an M3 MacBook Air — measured by Ars Technica, after a fresh install of the macOS 27.0 beta.
  • Around 14 GB on an M1 MacBook Air — also Ars Technica, on a Mac in the 8 GB tier.

Individual users have reported more still: around 24 GB on a MacBook Pro with an M3 Pro, and just over 30 GB on a machine running the macOS 27 release candidate. Those last two are forum reports rather than tested measurements, so treat them as the top of the range rather than a typical result.

The spread isn't a bug in anyone's measuring. Newer chips get larger models, and the total depends on which features and languages your Mac has downloaded.

What changed in macOS 27

Two things, and both matter if you're reading an older guide — including the one that used to be on this page.

The master toggle is gone. Earlier versions of macOS had a single switch to turn Apple Intelligence off. macOS 27 removed it. What's left is around a dozen separate settings spread across several panes, a few of them inside Screen Time — and switching all of them off still doesn't remove the feature.

Turning things off doesn't give the space back. This is the part that catches people. On earlier versions of macOS, disabling Apple Intelligence let macOS remove the downloaded models, and the space eventually returned. On macOS 27 the models stay on disk. macOS also downloads them automatically after you install it, the next time the Mac is online.

So on macOS 27 the space is, in practice, a cost of the operating system rather than something you opt into.

Where to see it on your Mac

Open System Settings → General → Storage, then click the ⓘ button next to macOS. Apple Intelligence is listed there as its own line.

It's worth knowing why it appears where it does. The models are system assets, so they count toward macOS — or toward System Data on some versions — and never toward an app. That's one reason the System Data bar grew for a lot of people after upgrading, with no obvious explanation. If that bar is your actual problem, it usually has several causes at once: see what System Data really is and why macOS takes up so much space.

What you can get back

On macOS 26 and earlier: turn Apple Intelligence off in System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri. macOS can then remove the models, though the space can take a while to appear and some supporting files stay behind. One measurement on macOS 15.3.1 saw the figure fall from 5.21 GB to 3.03 GB — real, but not all of it.

On macOS 27: nothing, through Settings. The models sit in protected system locations, so you can't delete them by hand either, and you shouldn't try — removing files from under System Integrity Protection is how people end up reinstalling macOS.

There is also a third-party route. Open-source tools such as RemoveMacAI disable Apple Intelligence on macOS 27, remove the downloaded models and stop macOS fetching them again; reports put the saving at roughly 12 GB. We haven't tested it and we're not recommending it. Be clear-eyed about what it is: an unofficial tool changing system state that Apple has deliberately stopped exposing, which can break on any macOS update and may need redoing after each one. If 10–20 GB is the difference between a working Mac and a full one, it's a real option — just not a casual one.

For most people the better answer is that 15 GB elsewhere on the disk is easier to find than 15 GB of system models. Which is the next section.

See where the rest of your space went

Apple Intelligence is a fixed cost you mostly can't negotiate. Everything else on the disk you can. Old iPhone backups, Xcode's DerivedData, a Docker disk image, years of Downloads — those are usually far larger, and unlike the models they're yours to delete.

Scan your startup disk with DiskGarden and the big folders stand out as the widest petals. If you develop on this Mac, Xcode storage and Docker taking up space are the two places to look first — they routinely hold more than Apple Intelligence does.
