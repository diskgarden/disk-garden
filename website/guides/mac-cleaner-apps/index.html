Do you need a Mac cleaner app?

Cleaner apps promise a faster Mac and gigabytes of free space at the press of a button. Here's what they actually remove, why the space often comes back, and what works better when your disk is full.

What cleaner apps remove

Most "one-click" cleaners delete the same kinds of files: app and browser caches, logs, temporary files, language files inside apps, and leftover files from removed apps.

macOS already manages most of these. It clears temporary files on its own, rotates logs, and removes purgeable data when space runs low.

Why the space comes back

Caches exist to make apps faster. Delete them and apps rebuild them as you use them — so the space you "freed" slowly returns, and apps can feel slower in the meantime. That's why a cleaner can report gigabytes freed every week.

Some cleaners also remove things you may want: app language files break features in some apps, and deleting files in ~/Library blindly can reset an app's settings or data.

What actually frees space

On almost every full Mac, a few large items take most of the space: videos and projects, old iPhone backups, Xcode or Docker data, disk images in Downloads, a photo library. Removing one of them usually frees more than a cleaner ever will — and it stays freed.

The hard part is finding them. A disk space analyzer like DiskGarden shows every folder by size, so the big ones stand out in seconds. You decide what goes, review it in the Waste bin, and system files can't be removed by accident.

When cleaning caches does help

  • An app misbehaves and its support page says to clear its cache.
  • A developer cache (Homebrew, npm, Gradle, DerivedData) has grown to many gigabytes — see which caches are safe to delete.
  • You need a little space right now, for an update or an install.
